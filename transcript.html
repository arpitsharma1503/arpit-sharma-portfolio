00 At a glance

The problem
In a meeting you can listen or take notes. Doing both means missing something.
The idea
Let the Mac write everything down, quietly, while you stay in the conversation.
The promise
No bot joins the call, no audio is saved, and nothing is uploaded. Ever.

01 The itch

I kept typing while people were talking.

Design reviews, sprint planning, client calls. Every week, the same trade: listen properly, or write it down.

When I typed, I missed the nuance. When I listened, I forgot the details by the next morning. The tools that promised to help either sent a bot into the call or uploaded the audio to someone else’s server. Neither felt right for client work.

So I built the tool I wanted: one that sits in the menu bar, does its job on my own Mac, and gets out of the way.

The rule I built it aroundBe in the meeting. Let the Mac take the notes.

02 How it works

Three moments. You only do the first.

  1. 1

    Join your call

    Zoom or Teams, in the app or the browser, or Google Meet. About 2 seconds in, a red dot appears in the menu bar.

  2. 2

    It writes as people talk

    Lines appear live, with a time and a speaker label. You can glance at them, or ignore them completely.

  3. 3

    Leave, and it’s saved

    About 8 seconds after you hang up, the last words are finished and a text file is saved, sorted by date.

03 Find it later

“What did we decide about the launch?” Just search.

A transcript is only useful if you can find things in it. So search works in the meeting you’re in, and across every meeting you’ve had.

  • Words or a speakerSearch “checkout”, or a label like “Speaker B”, to follow one voice through the meeting.
  • Every meeting, one boxPick Search Past Transcripts… from the menu bar and type, for example, a name and a topic.
  • Nothing is indexedFiles are read fresh each time. No search index is saved, and only your Mac is searched.

04 Put it to work

The meeting ends. The work starts.

Every transcript is clean, timestamped text. Hand it to your own AI agent, like Claude, and let it do the follow-up.

Ask it to check what was agreed and turn it into work. One file in, three useful things out.

To-dos

Every action, who said they’d do it and by when, ready for your task list.

Minutes of meeting

Decisions, open questions and next steps in a short summary you can share.

Project documents

Requirements, user stories, release notes or a design brief for project development.

05 Private by design

Your meetings stay on your Mac.

  • On-device speech recognitionWhisper runs on your Mac’s own chip. Audio never leaves it.
  • No names, no voice profilesYour lines say “You”. Everyone else is “Speaker A (Unidentified)”, “Speaker B” and so on. The app never guesses who someone is. Add a name yourself only if you want to.
  • No audio savedSound is held in memory for a few seconds, turned into text, then thrown away. Only text is saved.
  • Nothing without consentGive a one-time promise that people know they’re being transcribed, or tick a box before each call. A red dot shows while it works.

Please be fairAlways tell the people in your meeting that it is being transcribed, and follow the rules at your workplace.

06 The small things

Small touches that make it trustworthy.

English and Hindi

Hindi stays in Devanagari and English stays English. Nothing is translated or reworded.

Your words, spelled right

Add your team’s terms, like Figma or sprint. Fix a word twice and it’s learned.

Your own voice

Your mic is transcribed as “You”, and ignored while you’re muted in the call.

Share your way

Export as Markdown, subtitles (SRT), Word or PDF. Files stay put until you share them.

Never loses much

The file is rewritten about every 20 seconds, so even a crash costs only a few lines.

Picks the right model

Large v3 Turbo is chosen for your Mac: accurate with accents and noise, and still fast.

SHA-256: e729f4c858b7af93054de61d6099ca7e184856eb6b1a86df4736d3117cfe326d

  1. Open the DMG and drag Meeting Transcript into Applications.
  2. The app is not notarised by Apple yet, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open it once, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Click the icon in the menu bar. Under Automatic transcription, give consent, keep Include my voice on and press Turn On. The speech models download once (about 590 MB).
  4. Allow the microphone and audio-recording prompts. Then just join a call.

Good to knowTranscripts are saved in Documents/Meeting Transcript, sorted by date. Use headphones so the app can tell your voice from the call.
