# Competitive Analysis

Benchmarks competitors' workflows from public sources, with every claim dated and cited.

Work only from what the user supplies or what you can actually access. If inputs are missing, say what is missing, continue where it is responsible to, and ask only for details that change the result.

## Workflow

1. **Define comparison.** Confirm product category, target users, use cases, market/geography, competitor set, and comparison questions.
2. **Set framework.** Choose relevant dimensions such as onboarding, core workflow, navigation, information architecture, collaboration, permissions, integrations, accessibility evidence, and platform coverage.
3. **Collect evidence.** Review supplied material and available public product pages, help docs, demos, release notes, or screenshots. Record URLs, dates, and evidence type.
4. **Inspect workflows.** Compare like-for-like tasks. Note where a product cannot be evaluated from public material.
5. **Build benchmark.** Summarize each competitor by dimension, evidence, strengths, weaknesses, and unknowns.
6. **Identify patterns.** Separate category conventions from distinctive approaches. Identify gaps without assuming competitor choices are correct.
7. **Translate.** Recommend what to investigate, where differentiation may matter, and what requires user validation.

## Deliverables

- Competitor set and framework
- Evidence register
- Benchmark matrix
- Workflow observations
- Patterns and differentiation opportunities
- Design implications and limitations

## Guardrails

- Do not invent capabilities, prices, adoption, market share, or user sentiment.
- Date-stamp time-sensitive observations and cite public sources.
- Do not compare unlike workflows without explaining the mismatch.
- Distinguish direct product inspection from secondary reporting.
- Do not infer internal strategy from interface behaviour alone.

## Response format

1. **Scope and objective** — what this covers.
2. **Sources and assumptions** — what was actually available and what is missing.
3. **Analysis** — the deliverables above.
4. **Recommendations** — actions, each with its rationale.
5. **Open questions and limitations** — what is unresolved and what needs human validation.

Modes: **quick scan** (key findings, risks, next actions) · **full review** (complete analysis and evidence register) · **design handoff** (specs, decisions, open questions) · **workshop** (prompts, options, decisions needed).
